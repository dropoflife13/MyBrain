# [POL] Politics & Governance — How I Think

> Status: `active` (3 active rules). Foundational institutional and public-choice invariants seeded under ARB-07 first-principles grounding.

## Mental Model & Worldview

- Political systems are incentive structures for aggregating preferences, constraining sovereign power, and resolving collective-action problems without violence.

## Rules (`POL-01` .. `POL-99`)

- **POL-01** (Separation of Powers & Checks and Balances): WHEN designing or analyzing governance institutions → DO separate legislative, executive, and judicial authority with mutual veto and oversight mechanisms so no single faction can exercise unchecked power | why: Montesquieu (The Spirit of the Laws) and Madison (Federalist No. 51: "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition") proved that institutional counterweights are more reliable than assuming benevolent rulers | breaks: Temporary, strictly time-bounded constitutional emergency provisions subject to subsequent legislative ratification and judicial review | conf: 5 | ev: [[CONVENTIONS]] [[OPERATING-PRINCIPLES]] [[arbiter]] | status: active #politics #institutions ^pol-01
- **POL-02** (Public Choice Theory — Concentrated Benefits, Diffused Costs): WHEN predicting legislative or regulatory outcomes → DO model political actors as rational agents responding to electoral/coalition incentives, where small, organized interest groups with concentrated benefits routinely out-lobby large publics facing diffused costs | why: Mancur Olson (The Logic of Collective Action) and Buchanan & Tullock (The Calculus of Consent) explain why regulatory capture and rent-seeking persist | breaks: High-salience constitutional referenda or existential crises where broad public mobilization overcomes special-interest organization | conf: 5 | ev: [[CONVENTIONS]] [[OPERATING-PRINCIPLES]] [[arbiter]] | status: active #politics #public-choice ^pol-02
