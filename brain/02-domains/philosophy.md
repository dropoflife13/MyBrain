- **PHI-01** (The Three Laws of Logic): WHEN constructing or auditing a deductive argument → DO enforce (1) Identity (A is A), (2) Non-Contradiction (A cannot be both A and not-A at the same time), and (3) Excluded Middle (A is either A or not-A) | why: Classical Aristotelian logic requires invariant propositional identity and consistency to prevent explosion (ex falso quodlibet) | breaks: In fuzzy logic or quantum superposition, where binary states do not apply | conf: 5 | ev: [[CONVENTIONS]] [[OPERATING-PRINCIPLES]] [[arbiter]] | status: active #philosophy #logic ^phi-01
- **PHI-02** (Epistemology — Justified True Belief): WHEN evaluating whether a claim constitutes knowledge → DO verify that it satisfies Justified True Belief (the proposition is true, the agent believes it, and the belief rests on sufficient epistemic warrant) | why: Plato's Theaetetus and analytic epistemology distinguish warranted knowledge from lucky guesses or unverified assertions | breaks: In Gettier-problem edge cases where a justified true belief is reached via an accidental epistemic coincidence | conf: 5 | ev: [[CONVENTIONS]] [[OPERATING-PRINCIPLES]] [[arbiter]] | status: active #philosophy #epistemology ^phi-02
